Dynamic Glyph

Privacy Policy

Dynamic Glyph collects nothing. The app has no server, no account and no analytics, and there is no networking code in it at all. What it reads and what it keeps stay on your iPhone.

Effective 18 October 2026.

1. What the app reads and keeps

While you have it running, the app reads your screen through iOS screen sharing, which you start yourself and which keeps the system recording indicator visible the whole time. Frames are examined in memory and thrown away; nothing is recorded unless you turn on one of the options below.

  • Hack results and gear readings are saved on the phone: the Glyphs and bonuses the game showed, which Glyphs it accepted, timings, portal level and faction, the items read from each drop, and a note of the app version, capture mode, game language and your time zone offset. No portal names, player names, addresses or location are stored in these records.
  • Pictures of the game’s item panels are saved on the phone with each gear reading. You can switch this off in the result options.
  • Session logs and screenshots are written only if you turn on bug reporting in Settings. Those screenshots can show anything on screen at the time, including your position on the game map.
  • Shape and sequence files you import are stored in the app’s own folder, which you can browse and delete in the Files app.

The app asks for screen sharing, permission to add to Photos (it never reads your library), local notifications, and Live Activities. It never asks for the camera, the microphone, your location, your contacts or tracking.

2. What it is used for

Only to do what you asked: to recognise the hack as it happens, to show the sequence in the floating window and the Dynamic Island, to count what each hack dropped, and to show your own history in Statistics. Recognition runs entirely on the iPhone, using Apple’s on-device tools. There is no profiling, no advertising and no tracking of any kind.

3. Who else sees it

Nobody. The data is not sent to the developer, and there are no analytics tools, crash reporters, advertising networks or third-party kits in the app.

One option moves data off the phone, and it is off until you turn it on: iCloud sync, in the Statistics options. With it on, your saved results and gear readings sync through your own private iCloud database to your other iPhones signed in to the same Apple Account. That database belongs to you; the developer cannot read it. Pictures of the screen and of item panels are never synced. Apple’s handling of iCloud is covered by Apple’s own privacy policy.

Anything else leaves the phone only when you send it: a gear CSV export, a bug or misread report, or a screen recording you save to Photos. You choose what to send, and to whom.

4. How long it is kept, and how to delete it

Saved results are limited to 1,000 hacks and 1,000 drops and to 90 days; older records drop off by themselves. Clear results, in the result options, deletes them immediately, and single records can be deleted from Statistics. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the phone. Logs, screenshots and imported files can also be deleted in the Files app.

If you turned iCloud sync on, turning it off stops further syncing but does not delete what is already in iCloud; remove that in the iPhone’s Settings app, under your name, iCloud, Manage Account Storage. As the developer holds no copy of your data, there is nothing to request from us.

5. Withdrawing consent

Stop screen sharing at any time, with Stop in the app or from the iOS recording indicator. Each option can be turned off separately in Settings and in the result options: reading results, reading gear drops, saving panel pictures, keeping results, bug reporting and iCloud sync. iOS permissions can be withdrawn at any time in the iPhone’s Settings app under Dynamic Glyph.

6. Children, changes and contact

The app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

If this policy changes, the new version appears on this page with a new effective date.
